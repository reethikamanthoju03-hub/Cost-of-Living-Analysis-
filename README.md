# Cost of Living Analysis — Tokyo, Bangkok, Hanoi & Houston

## Overview
This project analyzes and compares the cost of living across four cities — Tokyo, Japan; Bangkok, Thailand; Hanoi, Vietnam; and Houston, Texas — over a three-year period (2021–2023). The goal was to move beyond simple national assumptions and identify which local economic factors actually drive cost differences across regions.

**Team project (BUSI 3100.305, Group 6)** — I was responsible for structuring the dataset, building the weighted value-based ranking methodology, and designing the Excel dashboard and Tableau visualization.

## What's Included
- `Living Cost Data.xlsx` — Raw and structured cost data across housing, utilities & transportation, food & grocery, and lifestyle categories, with PivotTables, conditional formatting, and sparklines.
- `Cost of Living Dashboard_.twb` — Interactive Tableau dashboard visualizing cross-city cost trends.
- `Living_States_Project_Report.pdf` — Full written report, including methodology, findings, and conclusions.

## Methodology
Rather than ranking cities by raw total cost, I built a **weighted, value-based ranking methodology** that compares relative price differentials by category. This surfaces which cost differences actually matter (e.g., a $400/month rent gap vs. a $5–20 gym membership gap) instead of treating every dollar the same.

## Key Findings
- **Houston** was the most expensive city overall (**$1,698/month**), driven primarily by high rent and utility costs — challenging the team's initial hypothesis that Tokyo would top the list.
- **Hanoi** was the most affordable city overall (**$506/month**), with consistent cost decreases across multiple categories.
- **Tokyo's** expenses actually declined in several categories (notably rent and public transportation) by 2023.
- On a value-adjusted basis, the final ranking (most to least value) was: **Bangkok → Hanoi → Tokyo → Houston.**
